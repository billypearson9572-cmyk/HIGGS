My First 90 Days — Marketing / IT Administrator

Andrew Bird Hearing A practical plan for how I'd hit the ground running across both the marketing and IT sides of the role.

My approach

I don't believe in changing things for the sake of it. The first phase is about listening, learning and protecting what already works — then I build momentum on quick wins before taking on bigger projects. Everything below is mapped to the actual responsibilities in the job description: IT infrastructure, SEO/Google Ads/Analytics, Squarespace, social media, content/design, project management, and data-protection compliance.

🟢 Days 1–30 — Learn, audit, secure, and bank quick wins

Goal: understand the business, lock down the essentials, and ship a few visible improvements.

Marketing

  • Sit with the team and Andrew to understand the patient journey, the busiest services, and what's worked/not worked before.
  • Get full access to Google Analytics, Google Business Profiles, social accounts, Squarespace and the email tool; baseline the current numbers so we can measure progress.
  • Claim/verify every Google Business Profile across the 18 locations — fastest, free local-visibility win.
  • Build and start a 30-day social content calendar (consistent, on-brand posting).
  • Audit the Squarespace site for quick conversion fixes (click-to-call, clear "Book" button, trust signals).

IT

  • Inventory all hardware, software, accounts and network setup across HQ and satellite clinics.
  • Review data protection and backups — confirm patient (special-category) data is encrypted, access-controlled and backed up. Flag any urgent gaps immediately.
  • Check security basics: antivirus, updates, password hygiene, MFA on key accounts.
  • Become the friendly, reliable first point of contact for staff IT issues.

By day 30: Google profiles live and optimised, social posting consistent, a documented IT/security baseline, and any critical data-protection risks closed.

🟡 Days 31–60 — Build the systems and the brand

Goal: turn quick wins into repeatable systems and roll out a consistent, modern brand.

Marketing

  • Roll out the refreshed brand (logo, colours, templates) across web, social and print for one consistent look.
  • Launch a Reels-first social strategy — educational and patient-story video (e.g. ear-wax microsuction, "what to expect"), with consent.
  • Set up review automation (post-appointment SMS/email requests) and display reviews on the site.
  • Begin local SEO on the priority location pages (titles, meta, schema, local content).
  • Start email marketing: welcome flow + first monthly newsletter.

IT

  • Document standard clinic IT setup so every site is consistent and easy to support.
  • Implement/verify a tested backup & recovery routine.
  • Run a short staff cyber-awareness session (phishing, passwords, data handling).
  • Scope Cyber Essentials certification if not already held.

By day 60: consistent brand live everywhere, video content running, reviews growing automatically, location SEO underway, and a documented, more secure IT estate.

🔵 Days 61–90 — Grow, measure, and plan ahead

Goal: drive measurable growth and set up bigger projects.

Marketing

  • Launch geo-targeted local ads (Google Ads + Meta) around priority clinic towns, focused on high-intent services (ear wax removal, hearing tests). Start small, measure, scale what works.
  • Develop the B2B / industrial hearing-testing offer — landing page, one-page PDF for HR/H&S managers, LinkedIn outreach.
  • Produce a monthly performance report (web traffic, bookings, social growth, review count, ad ROI) so the value is always visible.
  • Plan the website refresh roadmap within Squarespace.

IT

  • Finalise Cyber Essentials / data-protection documentation.
  • Review software/licensing for cost savings and better tools.
  • Propose a 12-month IT & marketing roadmap aligned to business goals.

By day 90: measurable growth in local visibility, bookings and social reach; a live reporting rhythm; a credible B2B pipeline; and a secure, well-documented IT foundation with a clear plan for the year ahead.

How I'd measure success

Area What I'd track
Local visibility Google Business Profile views, calls, direction requests, "near me" rankings
Website Sessions, appointment enquiries, calls, conversion rate
Social Followers, reach, video views, engagement
Reputation Number of reviews, average rating, review velocity
Retention Re-test/upgrade bookings from email campaigns
IT/security Backup success rate, incidents, Cyber Essentials status, staff-training completion

The throughline: protect what works, fix the quick wins fast, and build systems that keep growing the business after the initial push.
