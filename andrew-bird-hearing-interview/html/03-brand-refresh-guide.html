Andrew Bird Hearing — Brand Refresh Concept

A light-touch modernisation — not a reinvention. It keeps everything patients already trust (the name, the "since 2007" heritage, the warmth) and gives it a consistent, professional, accessible system that works the same on a Squarespace page, an Instagram post and a printed leaflet.

These are concept assets I created to show how I'd approach the brand — a starting point for a conversation, not a finished imposed identity.

The idea behind the mark

The new mark shows sound being heard — a point of sound with waves radiating out, clean and instantly readable even at small sizes (a favicon, a social avatar, a leaflet corner). It's simple, medical-but-warm, and it scales perfectly because it's vector. It also nods to the surname Bird — a small, clear "call" travelling out into the world.

Colour palette

Role Colour Hex Why
Primary Deep teal #0E5A66 Trust, calm, clinical-but-human. Strong contrast for accessibility.
Secondary Mid teal #2E8B9B Supporting tone for subheads and accents.
Accent Warm amber #F2A65A Warmth and energy; draws the eye to calls-to-action.
Ink Near-black #1A2B2E Body text.
Paper Soft off-white #F7F4EF Warmer and easier on the eye than pure white.
Muted Slate grey #6B7B7E Captions, fine print.

Accessibility note: the teal-on-white and white-on-teal combinations meet WCAG AA contrast — important for an audience that skews older and may have visual as well as hearing needs. This is a deliberate, on-message choice for a healthcare brand.

Typography

  • Headings: Poppins or Montserrat (geometric, friendly, modern) — Semibold/Bold.
  • Body: Inter or Source Sans (highly legible at small sizes).
  • Both are free Google Fonts — zero licensing cost and natively supported in Squarespace.
  • Minimum body size 16px on web; generous line spacing. Legibility is a brand value here, not just a nicety.

Tone of voice

Warm, plain-English, reassuring. We talk about people and outcomes ("hear your grandchildren again"), not jargon ("32-channel digital processing"). Confident but never pushy. The reviews already speak this way — "charming, gentle and thorough" — so the brand voice should simply match the in-clinic experience.

Strapline: keep the trusted "Improving your quality of life" — or, for campaigns, a punchier line like "Hear life clearly."

How it holds together

The same three ingredients repeat everywhere — teal + amber, the sound-wave mark, and clear friendly type — so a Facebook post, a newspaper advert and a clinic poster all look unmistakably like Andrew Bird Hearing. That consistency is what turns 18 separate clinics into one recognisable brand.
